# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

Sea Snake — an iOS Snake game, SwiftUI + SpriteKit. Xcode project at `SnakeGame.xcodeproj`, source under `SnakeGame/`.

## Commands

The Xcode project is generated by [XcodeGen](https://github.com/yonaskolb/XcodeGen) from `project.yml` — it is not the source of truth. **Whenever you add, remove, move, or rename a `.swift` file (or anything under `SnakeGame/`), regenerate the project before building:**

```bash
xcodegen generate
```

Build for the simulator (swap the destination for whatever's booted; `xcrun simctl list devices` to see options):

```bash
xcodebuild -project SnakeGame.xcodeproj -scheme SnakeGame -destination 'id=<SIMULATOR_UDID>' build
```

or, without needing a booted device first:

```bash
xcodebuild -project SnakeGame.xcodeproj -scheme SnakeGame -destination 'generic/platform=iOS Simulator' build
```

There is no test target yet.

## Architecture

### SwiftUI shell vs. SpriteKit game

The app is split cleanly at the framework boundary: SwiftUI owns navigation and chrome, SpriteKit owns the game itself.

- `SnakeGameApp` → `RootView` → either `StartView` or `ContentView`, switched on `@State private var isPlaying`. Switching is a hard swap (`if/else`, not a `NavigationStack`), so a fresh `ContentView` — and so a fresh `GameScene` and `GameSessionState` — is created every time Play is tapped. Nothing about a previous run persists across a re-entry to the game.
- `ContentView` hosts a single `SpriteView(scene:)`. From there down, SpriteKit's retained-mode scene graph takes over: nodes are mutated in place, not redrawn from scratch each frame.
- Game-over is *not* handled inside the scene. `GameScene` exposes a plain `onGameOver: (() -> Void)?` closure; `ContentView` wires that into `@StateObject private var session: GameSessionState` (`@Published var isGameOver`), which drives a SwiftUI `GameOverOverlay` shown over the `SpriteView`. Restart and "go home" are both SwiftUI-side actions (`scene.startNewGame()` / a closure back up to `RootView`) — swiping never restarts the game.
- The score HUD is SwiftUI too, the same way: `GameScene.onScoreChanged` → `session.score` → `ContentView`'s `ScoreHUD` pill (and the final score on `GameOverOverlay`). Keeping all chrome in SwiftUI is what lets it share the menu's fonts, glows and pills, and means nothing has to be reparented onto Adventure's camera to stay on screen.

### Look and feel: one deep-sea style, shared through `Theme/`

Everything follows `sea-snake-style-guide.md` (palette as color assets, Cinzel for display text, Work Sans for small labels). Pieces used by more than one screen live in `Theme/`:

- **`AbyssWaterNode`** — the moving water: one `SKSpriteNode` running an `SKShader` (domain-warped murk, light shafts, caustics, vignette, optional lightning `flash`) that recreates `SeaBackground`'s style-guide gradient inside the shader — keep the two in step. `energy` scales the churn: 1 on the menu, 0.45 behind the game board so pieces stay readable. Noise is scaled in points, so it looks the same on a screen-sized node or a map-sized one. It's an `SKShader` (GLSL, compiled at runtime) rather than a SwiftUI `.metal` shader **on purpose**: Xcode 26 won't build `.metal` files without a separate Metal Toolchain download, and this keeps the project building on stock Xcode.
- **`AmbientClock`** — the one clock all ambient motion reads (menu and game water alike). Counts from first use rather than `timeIntervalSinceReferenceDate`, whose magnitude would make the shader's 32-bit floats steppy.
- **`SeaBackground`** (the static gradient; also the Reduce Motion fallback), **`FloatingTitle`** (carved floating letters; takes its `lines`, so the overlays reuse it for "GAME OVER" / "LEVEL CLEARED") and **`DiveButtonStyle`** (the orb; the game-over "DIVE AGAIN" reuses it at a smaller `diameter`).

### The start menu is animated, but stateless

`StartView`'s menu-only pieces live in `App/StartMenu/`. Same approach as the math game's menu (`~/Desktop/math_addition_game`): every moving thing is a pure function of one clock, `AmbientClock.seconds(at:)`, drawn fresh each frame inside a `TimelineView` + `Canvas` — nothing is stored or stepped, and anything "random" (specks, bubbles, the serpent's route for a given pass) comes from `AmbientClock.random(index, salt:)`.

- **`LivingSea`** — the backdrop: `AbyssWaterScene` (a SpriteKit scene hosting one full-energy `AbyssWaterNode`, plus `LivingSea.lightning(at:)` flashes), and on top a `Canvas` of marine snow, blinking plankton, rising bubbles and `LurkingSerpent`.
- **`LurkingSerpent`** — a giant silhouette that crosses every 32 s (24 s swimming, 8 s of empty water). Passes alternate between the two stretches of open water the layout leaves (above the title, and between the title and the mode pill); moving the title or pill means re-checking those `baseY` fractions, or it'll swim behind the UI. It reads against the dark water mostly through its blurred dark aura and the rim light along its back, not its body fill.
- **`FloatingTitle`** — per-letter bob/rock on a swell, breathing glow (the style guide's title glow values), carved depth layers, and a periodic light sweep.
- **`DiveButtonStyle`** — the "DIVE IN" orb (copy from the style guide): heartbeat glow, sonar rings, turning rings. Its glow/rings draw in a `.background` that spills past the frame so they don't affect layout; only the orb's circle is tappable. Tapping it plays a 0.7 s zoom-and-fade (`StartView.dive()`) before calling `onPlay`.
- **Reduce Motion**: the water falls back to the static `SeaBackground` (a `SpriteView` that starts paused never renders a frame, so don't just pause it) and the canvases freeze at `AmbientClock.still`; the dive skips its animation.

### Mode selection flows downward, ahead of geometry

`GameMode` (`.freePlay` / `.levels` / `.adventure`, `CaseIterable` so new modes don't need toggle-logic changes — `StartView`'s mode pill just calls `mode.next` and shows `mode.title`/`mode.tagline`) is chosen on `StartView` and owned by `RootView`. It has to reach `GameScene` *before* `didMove(to:)` runs, because the grid's `columns`/`rows` (and therefore `cellSize`/`origin`) depend on the mode:

- Free-play: fixed 16×24, centered, square cells sized to fit the screen less a 14pt margin, with the style guide's board panel (`SpriteArt.board`) marking the wrap boundary. The margin is there for the panel: at 16 columns the grid would otherwise run edge to edge and the panel's rounded edge and glow would spill off screen.
- Levels: 16×30, same fit-to-screen sizing, no border (already fills most of the screen), sized from whichever level a run starts on.
- Adventure: a fixed 24pt cell size (`GameScene.adventureCellSize`) instead of fit-to-screen — the grid is deliberately *larger* than the viewport (see below), so there's nothing to fit.

That's why `GameScene` takes `mode` as an init parameter rather than being constructed bare and configured after the fact.

### GameManager orchestrates; each grid entity owns its own state + rendering

`GameManager` is the only thing that knows about scoring, collisions between different entity types, and game-over. It does *not* own rendering directly — `Snake`, `Food`, `PowerUp`, `Bomb`, and `Walls` each manage their own `SKNode`(s) and expose `addToScene(_:)`/`removeFromScene()`. This split matters for two reasons:

1. **Collision ordering encodes what can protect you.** `Snake.advance(...)` doesn't know food, bombs, walls, or power-ups exist. Before moving it asks a caller-supplied `isBlocked` about the next cell (`GameManager.isBarrier`: walls and the kraken's lair) and treats that, like its own body, as a barrier it stops against (`.blocked`) rather than enters. Everything else — food-eaten, bomb contact, power-up pickup — is resolved by `GameManager` *after* `advance()` commits the move, by comparing `snake.head` against each entity's position. The order those checks run in is deliberate: power-up pickup resolves before bomb-collision in the same tick, so grabbing bomb-eater and hitting a bomb in one move grants immunity for that same collision rather than costing a life a beat early. Barriers have no such interplay (nothing grants immunity to them).
2. **Rendering strategy differs by how often a thing changes.** `Snake` reuses/repositions its segment nodes every tick rather than destroying and rebuilding them (`syncNodes()`) — worth preserving as things get more visually complex. `Food`/`PowerUp`/`Bomb`/`Walls` are simpler since they don't move once placed (or, for `Food`/`PowerUp`, just get replaced wholesale on relocate/respawn).

**Everything on the board is an `SKSpriteNode` with a texture from `SpriteArt`**, drawn once per cell size with Core Graphics and cached: glowing gradient snake segments, the head and its face, the fruit (a glowing orb in the original red→orange mango gradient, kept on purpose over the style guide's pearl), power-up orbs (color + SF Symbol), horned sea mines (a light in the kind's color; the sensor's is ringed), the barrel, reef-rock wall tiles (a few variants, picked by position) and the free-play board. Don't go back to `SKShapeNode` with `glowWidth` for anything numerous — it re-renders a blur every frame and shape nodes can't batch (Adventure alone has hundreds of wall cells). Glows are baked into the textures, which are padded by `SpriteArt.glowPad` on every side, so a sprite is still centered on its cell. A few snake details:

- The head texture faces right and `syncNodes()` sets its `zRotation` from `direction`. Its face is a separate child sprite (`SpriteArt.snakeFace`), so a power-up tint washes over the head without tinting the eyes. The head and face deliberately depart from the style guide's big white friendly eyes and pale-gold whiskers, toward a menacing sea serpent rather than a mascot: the head is wider than the neck at the temples and tapers to a blunt snout with nostrils (`serpentHeadPath`; a plain rounded shape read as a worm), and the eyes are slanted dark sockets holding only a pale-gold glow — no pupils — matching the menu serpent's eye. The slant does the glaring: brows were tried and read as cartoon eyebrows.
- Power-up tints are SpriteKit color blending (`SegmentSprite.style(tint:glow:)`, `colorBlendFactor` 0.7) over the normal art, not separate textures; the bomb-eater grace glow is an additive halo sprite behind each segment.
- Body segments are drawn a touch bigger than their cell, so neighbors overlap into one body, and fade slightly toward the tail. The last `tailTaperLength` segments narrow toward the tail — scaled only *across* the body (`xScale`/`yScale` picked from the step toward the head, unwrapping grid-edge wraps), never along it, so no gaps open — and the very last swaps to `SpriteArt.snakeTail`, a point rotated to aim away from the body and tucked under the segment before it. A plain rounded head and blunt square tail read as a worm.

### Steering

Continuous drag (`GameScene.handlePan`) sets `Snake.pendingDirection`; the tick commits it in `Snake.advance`. Two rules guard it:
- `Snake.turn(to:)` rejects a direct reversal of the last direction the snake *moved* (its neck).
- `resolveNextDirection` drops the third consecutive turn the same way (left-left-left or right-right-right over consecutive ticks). Two in a row is a legitimate quick U-turn; a third hooks back into the body. This was the fix for [#7](https://github.com/fedxemilio/snake_game_app/issues/7), where fast circular drags chained into a reversal.
- A tick that ends `.blocked` commits nothing: the turn doesn't count toward the streak, the snake keeps facing the way it last moved, and the requested turn stays pending. If the streak rule drops a turn but straight ahead is blocked, the turn is taken anyway when it's open. The rule exists to prevent accidental hooks, not to pin the snake against a wall.

### Lives and damage

Every way the snake gets hurt goes through one function, `GameManager.hurtPlayer(_:)`, told what did it (`Hurt`: a barrier at a cell, a bomb, a blast, a tentacle, a bite). The causes are a bump into a wall, the sky, a boss or its own body (a `.blocked(at:)` tick), touching a bomb, a sensor blast, a barrel blast, a tentacle strike, the moray's bite and a cannon shot landing (a blast). It costs a life, unless `hitGraceRemaining` is still running from the last hit, in which case it's ignored. Callers check `isGameOver` afterwards to decide whether to carry on.
- **Not the last life**: `showImpact` (a puff where the head met the barrier, or a red splash for a tentacle; bombs and blasts bring their own explosions), `Snake.playHitImpact` (every segment flashes red, the snake shudders), a `.hit` jolt, then `hitGraceDuration` (2 s of real time, ticked every frame like bomb-eater) of `Snake.playHitBlink`. The lost heart pops with a red flash in `LivesHUD` (`HeartIcon`'s keyframe animation).
- **The last life**: `isGameOver` is set at once, so the loop and input stop, but `onGameOver` (and so the overlay) waits for `Snake.playDeath`'s completion: a convulsion and white flash, then the snake bursts into bubbles head to tail, about 1.3 s. This is the only place `isGameOver` is set.
- Power-up effects, the blink and the hit/death shudder each run on `Snake`'s container under their own action key, so none cancels another. Flashes are additive overlay sprites on each segment (`SegmentSprite.flash`), so they show over a power-up tint without disturbing it.
- **Jolts** (`GameManager.onJolt`: `.hit`, `.death`, `.explosion(point)`) become camera shake in Adventure (`GameScene.shake`, added on top of the clamped camera position each frame; skipped under Reduce Motion; explosions off screen ignored) and haptics.

- **Lives by mode**: `maxLives` is 3 in Adventure and 1 elsewhere, so Free play and Levels play exactly as before (any hit ends the run). `onLivesChanged` reports nil when there's only one life, which hides the hearts.
- **Barriers stop the snake**: walls, the kraken's lair, its own body, and a pushable that can't be shoved (see "Pushable objects"). It stays put, facing the barrier, and every tick it's still pressing in calls `hurtPlayer()`, which only lands once the grace period is over. So sitting against a wall costs a life every `hitGraceDuration` until the player turns somewhere open. It can't turn back into its own neck (`Snake.turn(to:)`), so a dead end with no open side keeps costing lives.
- **Bombs go off when touched**: they explode either way (`Bomb.explode(in:)`), costing a life unless bomb-eater is active or the snake is in its grace period. The exception is a bomb a barrel is primed on, which is spent and harmless (see "Pushable objects").
- **HUD**: `GameScene.onLivesChanged` → `session.lives` → `ContentView`'s `LivesHUD`, teal hearts in a pill under the score (beside it, the pill would run under the Dynamic Island). Replays on set, like the boss's lives.
- While blocked, the kraken aims strikes at the snake itself rather than ahead of it (`isSnakeBlocked`).

### Zen mode (for testing)

A toggle at the bottom of the start menu, shown only in Debug builds (`#if DEBUG` in `StartView`), stored with `@AppStorage("isZenMode")` in `RootView` so it stays on between launches, and passed down like `mode` (`ContentView` → `GameScene` → `GameManager.isZen`).

- **Nothing costs a life**, in any mode: `hurtPlayer` plays a hit as usual (impact, flash, blink, grace period) but skips the life, so `isGameOver` is never set. `ContentView` shows a teal `ZenHUD` badge where the hearts would be.
- **Any Adventure stage can be the start**: on the opening chart (`voyage.from == nil`), `VoyageChart.onPickStage` makes the stops tappable. Picking one retargets the chart's leg and calls `GameManager.chooseStartingStage(_:)`, which loads that stage behind the chart while the run is still `isWaitingToStart` (via `enterStage()`, shared with `beginNextStage()`). The chart only ticks stops actually beaten (`index <= voyage.from`), not skipped ones.

### Bomb kinds

`BombKind` (in `Bomb.swift`) is `.mine` / `.timed` / `.drifter` / `.sensor` / `.barrel`. `GameManager.attemptBombSpawn()` picks one per spawn via a weighted roll (`bombKindWeights`). `Bomb` owns its own per-kind behavior rather than `GameManager` branching on kind. **Currently `.mine`/`.sensor`/`.barrel` are live** — `.timed`/`.drifter` are shelved at 0 weight while `.sensor` (and its barrel interaction) get hands-on testing at an intentionally-overweighted split; the plan is to drop `.sensor` to ~20% once that's confirmed working. All five kinds' code stays in place regardless of current weights.

- `.mine` is the original hazard — permanent, stationary, never expires.
- `.timed` counts down a real-seconds fuse (`Bomb.update(delta:...)`, ticked every frame like the bomb-eater timer so it isn't tied to grid speed) and fades itself out once it hits zero, pulsing faster in its final `fuseWarningThreshold` seconds as a warning. `GameManager.updateBombs(delta:)` sweeps expired ones out of the `bombs` array right after ticking them, before that tick's snake-vs-bomb collision check runs.
- `.drifter` never expires but takes a step to a random open neighboring cell (wrapping at the grid's edges the same way the snake does) every `driftInterval` real seconds, via the same per-frame `update(delta:...)` call. It reuses the snake's own occupied-cell/wrap logic rather than duplicating a separate movement system.
- `.sensor` starts **dormant**: inert, calm pulse, harmless even if the snake passes right next to it. It also carries a `vicinityIndicator` node — a faintly shaded square (no outline) over its 3x3 blast area, so the danger zone reads at a glance instead of only being obvious after arming. `GameManager.armSensorBombsNearHead()` runs once per grid tick and calls `Bomb.arm()` on any dormant sensor whose 8 surrounding cells (its "vicinity", via `GameManager.vicinity(of:columns:rows:)`) contain the snake's head. `arm()` is a no-op on anything already armed or not a sensor, so it's called unconditionally rather than tracked. Once armed, it switches straight to the fast/blinking pulse (and deepens the vicinity indicator's shading) and counts down `sensorFuseDuration` — there's no calm-then-warn runway like `.timed` gets, since arming already means the player got close. When that fuse hits zero, `GameManager.resolveSensorDetonation(at:)` runs, before that bomb is swept: if the snake's head is in the vicinity (and bomb-eater isn't active), it costs a life (and the chain reaction below still plays out unless that was the last one); any `.mine` bombs in the vicinity are removed outright; any other dormant `.sensor` bombs in the vicinity are armed too (so one detonation can chain into a cascade of others, each on its own subsequent fuse); and any `.barrel` in the vicinity explodes (see below).
- `.barrel` is a squarish banded cask rather than a round horned mine (`Bomb`'s init branches its texture on kind — the one deliberate silhouette break), and it isn't a hazard to touch: it's `Pushable` (see below). It sits still (no idle pulse, drawn above any bomb it's on) until something sets it off: a `.sensor` detonating within its vicinity, or its own fuse after being pushed onto a bomb. Either way `GameManager.detonate(_:)` runs: `Bomb.explodeBarrel(columns:rows:scene:)` blasts beams down its entire row and column, `GameManager` checks that same row/column against `snake.head` (`GameManager.cross(at:contains:)`) — a hit there costs a life, but the explosion passing through the snake's body/neck is harmless, since only the head is checked — and against the kraken's lair, and the bomb it was primed on goes off with it.

**Explosions** (`BoardEffects`: flash, light on the water, shockwave, casing shards, bubbles, all self-removing sprites) play for every way a bomb goes off. A touched bomb, or a mine cleared by a sensor, gets `Bomb.explode(in:delay:)`; cleared mines are staggered just behind the sensor's blast so a chain reaction reads in order. An armed sensor's fuse ending gets `BoardEffects.sensorBlast` (its 3×3 area flares). A barrel gets `crossBlast` (beams down its whole row and column, two stretched sprites rather than a shape per cell). A timed bomb's harmless fuse-out gets a soft `fizzle`, deliberately not a blast.

`GameManager.updateBombs(delta:)`'s expired-bomb sweep only removes entries from the `bombs` array — it deliberately does *not* also call `removeFromScene()` on them. Every path that sets `isExpired` (a `.timed`/`.sensor` fuse reaching zero, or `.barrel.explodeBarrel(...)`) already queues that bomb's own fade/scale-out `SKAction` on its node, and so does `Bomb.explode(in:)` (for bombs `GameManager` drops from `bombs` itself), so none of those paths call `removeFromScene()` either; since SpriteKit evaluates actions *after* `update(_:)` returns, calling `removeFromScene()` in the same pass would yank the node out before that action ever gets to run a single frame, so the fade would never actually be visible. The node keeps animating and removes itself once its own action sequence finishes, independent of whether `bombs` still references its `Bomb` wrapper.

Because `.timed`/`.drifter`/`.sensor`'s countdowns are driven by real time rather than grid ticks, none of this needed to touch `Snake.advance(...)` — `updateBombs(delta:)` sits alongside `updateBombEaterTimer(delta:)`, called unconditionally every frame before the `timeSinceLastMove` gate. The one exception is arming, which is proximity- (not time-) based and so is checked once per grid tick instead, from the same block as the wall/power-up/bomb-contact checks.

**Bomb spawn fairness** (`GameManager.bombSpawnPosition()`): a new bomb never lands in the head's reaction zone — `bombReactionAhead` cells ahead (including the cells level with the head) by `bombReactionSide` to each side, wrapping like the snake does; nothing is reserved behind the head. In Adventure mode it also avoids cells within `adventureBombEdgeMargin` of a map edge, but only along rows/columns the snake can actually wrap through (a wall at either end of that row/column blocks the crossing, so it doesn't count) — the viewport can't show the far side of a wrap. This is a logical stopgap: revisit if a "sensor" ever reveals the far side of a wrap, or if walls become breakable. Only spawn placement is affected; `.drifter` movement isn't constrained by it.

### Pushable objects

`Pushable` (in `Pushable.swift`) is something the snake shoves out of its way instead of stopping against or eating. Anything new that should move this way conforms and gets listed in `GameManager.pushables`. Each declares a `pushStyle`, whether it's `isSolid` right now, and whether it `landsOnBombs`.

- **Barrels** (`Bomb` with kind `.barrel`) **step**: one cell per push, so the snake walks one along in front of its head.
- **Cannonballs** (`Cannonball`) **roll**: one push sends one off until something stops it. Bombs stop them too (they don't set them off). Two bosses take damage from them (`GameManager.cannonballTarget`: the galleon's hull and anywhere along the moray; the kraken ignores them). `push` checks the cell just past where the roll would end, and if it's one of that boss's `cells`, `fire` sends the ball to `smash` into it: it's gone from `cannonballs` at once (so the snake moves into the cell it left; point blank works too), and the hit lands as it arrives (`cannonHits`, counted down in `updateCannonHits`, then `strike`). Breaking is deliberate: the moray's stage has only two, so they can't win that fight on their own.
- **Pushing.** Solid pushables count as barriers (`GameManager.isBarrier`), so running into one makes `Snake.advance` return `.blocked(at:)`. The tick then tries `push(_:awayFrom:)`; if the object moved, it calls `advance` again, and the snake moves into the cell it left. A blocked advance commits nothing, so the retry resolves the same move.
- **Where it goes** (`route(of:heading:)`): one cell for a stepper, on and on for a roller, stopping before a barrier (wall, lair, another solid pushable), the snake, the power-up or the map's edge. Pushables don't wrap like the snake: a roll around an open row would never end. Fruit doesn't stop them: any fruit one touches on the way (every cell it passes through or stops on) counts as eaten by the snake (`collectFruit(along:)`): the mango scores and grows it (`Snake.feed(halvedGrowth:)`, which owes a segment to its next move, Levels' halving included), and mine fruit counts toward a mine. Gameplay treats the object as at its destination at once, and the move is animated.
- **Can't move at all**: it's as solid as a wall, and bumping it costs a life like one.
- **A barrel pushed onto a bomb** lands on top of it and primes (`Bomb.prime(on:after:)`): it blinks hot for `barrelFuseDuration` (1.2 s, short on purpose; getting off its row and column in time is part of the puzzle), then `GameManager.updateBombs` detonates it, taking the bomb underneath along. From the moment it primes, the pair is spent: the barrel isn't `isSolid`, and the bomb is `isUnderBarrel` (harmless to touch, won't arm). So the snake can swim straight through that cell; only the blast itself can hurt it.
- Pushables are folded into `occupiedBySnakeAndWalls`, so nothing spawns or drifts onto one.

### Mine fruit and dropped mines (Adventure)

Adventure keeps a second fruit on the board, `GameManager.mineFruit` (a `Food` of kind `.mineFruit`, drawn as a glowing crimson urchin), placed and replaced just like the mango. It doesn't grow the snake, score or speed it up. Every `mineFruitPerMine` (3) eaten earns a mine (`minesReady`, which banks), and a tap anywhere (`GameScene.handleTap` → `GameManager.dropMine()`; a tap never travels, so it doesn't compete with the steering drag) drops one on the head's cell. That's safe: only a head ever touches a mine, the mine check runs only after the snake moves, and the body passing over is harmless. Progress and mines ready reach SwiftUI as `MineSupply` (`onMineSupplyChanged` → `session.mineSupply` → `ContentView`'s `MineHUD`, under the hearts; it turns into a glowing "TAP ×n" once one's ready). This is how the player places mines to push barrels onto.

### Grid coordinate convention

`GridPoint(x: 0, y: 0)` is the **bottom-left** cell. `y` increases upward, matching `Direction.up.vector == (0, 1)` and SpriteKit's own y-up coordinate system — `GridGeometry.position(for:cellSize:origin:)` is the single place grid coordinates become screen points, and everything (`Snake`, `Food`, `PowerUp`, `Bomb`, `Walls`) routes through it rather than computing its own. `Level.walls` has to flip its ASCII map's row order to honor this (the map's first string is visually the top row, i.e. the *highest* `y`).

### Game loop is tick-based, not per-frame

`GameScene.update(_:)` runs every frame (~60/sec) and forwards to `GameManager.tick(currentTime:)`, but grid movement only advances once `timeSinceLastMove` accumulates past `moveInterval` — the standard "decouple game logic from frame rate" pattern. `moveInterval` is driven purely by fruit eaten (it has no relationship to snake length; confirmed independent since `tail-cutter` shrinks the snake without touching it). Timed effects like bomb-eater's ~5-second immunity are tracked separately in real elapsed time (`bombEaterTimeRemaining -= delta`, decremented every frame) rather than in grid ticks, so they don't speed up or slow down with the snake.

### Levels

`Level` (in `Level.swift`) defines a layout as an ASCII grid (`-` empty, anything else a wall) plus `pointsToAdvance`, mirroring the shape of the original Python prototype's `LEVELS` list. `Level.all` is the fixed roster of three; all three currently share one grid size since `cellSize` is computed once per run and can't change without rebuilding the scene. `GameManager` tracks level advancement via a *separate* `levelProgress` counter, not the run's total `score` — thresholds compare against `levelProgress`, which resets to 0 on every advance, specifically so cycling back to level 1 after the last level doesn't instantly re-trigger every threshold against an already-high total score. On advance, walls/food/bombs/power-up are swapped/cleared, but the snake and total score carry over.

Levels mode also halves the snake's real growth rate (`Snake.advance(..., halvedGrowth:)`): only every *other* fruit actually lengthens `segments`, tracked via `Snake.pendingGrowth` toggling each fruit. The in-between fruit shows a small translucent circle trailing the tail as a preview of the growth to come — deliberately **not** a real grid cell (never added to `segments`, never touches the collision check in `advance`), just a cosmetic node whose position is recomputed each frame by extrapolating the last two real segments. That was a deliberate choice to avoid needing any half-collidable-cell logic. Free play and adventure mode are unaffected (`halvedGrowth: mode == .levels` is the only gate).

### Adventure mode: a world bigger than the screen

Free-play and levels both size their grid to fit entirely on screen. Adventure mode inverts that: every stage's map (`AdventureMap`) is 70×40, deliberately larger than any phone viewport at a legible cell size. All stages share that one size, because the scene's geometry (cell size, world size, camera bounds, the map-sized water) is set once per run; stages swap what's in the world, never its size. Consequences that ripple through `GameScene`:

- An `SKCameraNode` (`GameScene.cameraNode`, non-nil only in `.adventure`) is what the player actually sees through; `GameScene.updateCamera()` runs every frame (not just on grid ticks) and re-centers it on `GameManager.cameraFocus`, clamped so the viewport never shows past the map's edges — same clamp-camera-to-world-bounds idea as the Python prototype's `camera_x`/`camera_y`. The focus is the head, except in a stage with sky, where it's pinned to the top of the map so the surface and the galleon are always in view (the map is only ~4 rows taller than the screen, so that stage's map makes its bottom 4 rows solid seabed rather than leave water you can't see).
- The water (`AbyssWaterNode`) is sized to the whole map, not the screen, and stays put as the camera moves over it — so it drifts past as the snake swims and gets darker toward the bottom of the map, like real depth. The marine-snow emitter rides along the top of the camera's view but leaves its specks in the world (`targetNode = scene`) so they scroll past too. The score HUD is SwiftUI, so it needs no camera handling.
- `AdventureMap.walls(in:)` only treats `'0'` as a wall, and `sky(in:)` reads `'~'` as sky above the surface (stage 3). Sky blocks exactly like a wall (it goes into `Walls.positions`, so barriers, spawns and the wrap-edge check all treat it the same) but isn't drawn as rock (`Walls(..., drawn:)`); `StormSurface` draws it. Stage 1's map was ported from a second, unrelated Python prototype's `map_data.py`, whose `'1'`/`'c'` were floor-texture tiles (path/curve) with no gameplay meaning here. Unlike that script (whose walls were decorative — it never checked wall collision at all), these walls are real and blocking, reusing the same `Walls` type as Levels mode.
- Otherwise: standard (non-halved) growth, wrap-around at the *map's* true edges where they're open (stage 2's map is walled all round), bombs/power-ups unchanged from free-play.

### Adventure's stages and bosses

Adventure is an ascent from the abyss toward the surface: a run of `AdventureStage.all`, each a map, a boss, a snake start and fixtures (barrels, cannonballs, `startingMines`). `GameManager.stageIndex` tracks the current one; `loadAdventureStage()` swaps walls, boss and fixtures (clearing every bomb); the snake, fruit and lives are handled around it. Adventure's snake starts at `stage.snakeStart` rather than the center, since the bosses live there.

- **The flow.** A run opens on the voyage chart (`VoyageChart`, from the abyss to stage 1): `GameScene.didMove` starts the game with `holdingForCue`, which freezes the board (`isWaitingToStart`) until BEGIN calls `releaseStart()`. A Dive Again restart skips the chart. Beating a boss starts `stageClearCountdown` (3 s, so its send-off plays while the game runs on), then `isAwaitingNextLevel` freezes the game and `onStageCleared` fires. SwiftUI shows `VictoryOverlay` (the boss's name over the stage's `victoryWord`, "SLAIN" or "SUNK", and its `victoryLine`), then the chart's next leg; SWIM ON fades to black (`ContentView.isBetweenStages`) while `beginNextStage()` loads the next stage. Score, length, speed and banked mines carry over; lives refill. Past the last stage, the chart's final leg climbs from the last stop up through the surface line, and it offers Dive Again (a fresh run from stage 1) or Surface (the menu).
- **`Boss`** (in `Moray.swift`) is what `GameManager` needs from any boss: `lives`, `isDefeated`, `cells` (barrier cells — the snake stops against them and it costs a life; also folded into `occupiedBySnakeAndWalls`), `isCrossed(byBlastFrom:)` and `takeHit()`. Every hit goes through `GameManager.strike(_:)`, which reports `BossStatus` (name, lives, max) → `GameScene.onBossChanged` → `session.boss` → `ContentView`'s `BossHUD`, and starts the stage clear on the last life. `GameScene` replays the last status whenever that closure is set, because the first report comes from `didMove(to:)`, which can run before SwiftUI attaches its listener.
- **The chart** (`VoyageChart`) draws a route through fixed waypoints (the abyss, one per stop, and the surface at the end), sampled so the marker (`Traveler`, an `Animatable` view) can be placed at any fraction of its length; each stop's icon is its boss's actual `SpriteArt` texture (`SKTexture.cgImage()`). Adding a stage means adding a waypoint (the chart is laid out for the current three) and an icon case.
- **Aim.** Both warned attacks (tentacle strikes, cannon shot) aim at `GameManager.aimAhead(by:)`: where the head will be after the warning if it keeps going straight, or where it is while it's stopped against something. Aiming at the current cell doesn't work: at normal speed the snake swims ~11 cells during a 2 s warning.

**Stage 1, Kraken's Lair: the kraken** (`Kraken.swift`), centered at its stage's `lair`.

- **The lair** (`Kraken.footprint`, a disc of radius `lairRadius` ≈ 9 cells across) is its `cells`. It empties once the kraken is defeated.
- **Tentacle strikes** (`TentacleStrike`) run on real time, ticked from `GameManager.updateKraken(delta:)` right after `updateBombs` (same "not tied to grid speed" reasoning as the bomb timers). Each is a 9×9 patch: a red warning glow (the sensor's transparent, outline-free style, kept strong enough that red over teal doesn't read as grey) flashing faster for `warningDuration`, then a tentacle that bursts up, sweeps once around and sinks. The **whole patch hurts while the tentacle is up** (`isLethal`), **head only**, same rule as a barrel blast; bomb-eater doesn't protect (tentacles aren't bombs). It costs one life, and the 2 s grace period outlasts the ~1.2 s the tentacle is up, so one strike can't take two. It's checked every frame, not every tick, since lethality changes between ticks.
- **Strike placement is aimed ahead** (`aimAhead`): the kraken picks a random patch around the aim point (`strikeReachX/Y`), clamped inside the map and a cell clear of the lair.
- **Fixtures**: a line of barrels down the left side (one at the head of every other row from 11 to 25) and three cannonballs on the right. There are no fixed mines: the player makes them from mine fruit, drops them, then walks a barrel onto one on a row or column through the lair.
- **Damage**: only barrel blasts. In `detonate(_:)`, a barrel whose row or column crosses the footprint costs a life (`Kraken.maxLives` = 3). It flinches (flash, shudder, ink) and thrashes, and is immune for `hitInvulnerability` so one chain reaction can't take every life. At zero it sinks away and cancels its strikes.
- **Gameplay and drawing are separate.** `update(delta:aim:)` advances state (only while the game runs); `animate(time:focus:)` poses everything from `AmbientClock` every frame via `GameManager.animate(time:)`, even after a game over, so the arms keep flailing behind the overlay and the strike that hit you stays frozen mid-swing. The eyes' pupils follow `focus` (the head).
- **Drawing**: every arm, idle or striking, is a `TentacleChain`: overlapping `SpriteArt.tentacleLink` sprites (one shared texture, suckers on alternate links) posed each frame by integrating a curvature along the arm (a rolling wave plus a tip curl; striking arms add a lag against the swing). The body, eyes, pupils, ink and splash are `SpriteArt` textures too.

**Stage 2, Moray Hollows: the blind moray** (`Moray.swift`), in an enclosed trench of rock pillars, starting coiled in its cave at the top.

- **It hunts.** It moves on the same grid, one cell per `moveInterval` (slower than the snake at first, faster with each life lost: `moveIntervals`), after a `wakeDelay`. Each move is the first step of a breadth-first search to the snake's head over `isOpen` cells (`GameManager.isOpenForMoray`: not walls or solid pushables; yes to the snake and to mines). Its own body blocks the search, bar the tail. Ties break randomly; with no way through, it moves anywhere open rather than freezing.
- **It bites.** A step into any part of the snake is a bite (`Hurt.bite`) instead of a move: the head snaps forward, and it holds off for `biteRecoil`. Its body is its `cells`, so swimming into it hurts like a wall.
- **It's blind.** Mines don't exist to it, so a step onto any bomb sets the bomb off and costs it a life (`GameManager.updateMoray`). The intended play is to drop mines in its path: it follows the snake, so behind you works. A cannonball rolled into it anywhere along its body hurts it too, and breaks (see "Pushable objects"): the stage's two are worth two of its three lives at most, so mines are still needed. A barrel blast across its head (`isCrossed`, head only, like the snake) hurts it as well. Each hit flashes it, shakes it and stuns it for `hitStun`; at zero it comes apart into bubbles and stops being in the way at once.
- **Drawing**: a sprite per segment (head with an open jaw and needle teeth and milky blind eyes, two alternating body textures with glowing spots, a finned tail), each sliding to its new cell over the move interval, so it glides rather than hops; the head and tail turn to face along the body.
- **Fixtures**: four barrels and two cannonballs among the pillars, and one starting mine so the trick can be tried before any mine fruit.

**Stage 3, the Shallows: the ghost galleon** (`Galleon.swift`, `CannonShot.swift`), the last stage, just under the stormy surface. The top 8 rows of the map are sky; the galleon sails on the waterline.

- **The storm** (`StormSurface`, created by `loadAdventureStage` for any stage with sky): a gradient sky sprite over the sky rows, drifting cloud sprites, a tiled strip of wave crests scrolling along the waterline (above the galleon, so the waves lap at its hull), a rain emitter that falls only as far as the water, and lightning. `lightning(at:)` is a pure function of `AmbientClock` (a strike most `period`s: a flicker and an echo); it brightens the sky, shows a bolt near the snake, lights the clouds, and reaches the water shader's `flash` through `GameManager.lightning(at:)` → `GameScene`. Under Reduce Motion there's no rain or lightning and it holds still.
- **Sailing.** The galleon's `column` (the middle of a hull 9 cells long, `halfLength` 4) moves one cell toward the snake's column per sail interval (`sailIntervals`, faster as it's hurt), gliding there. More than `raceDistance` columns behind, it races at `raceInterval` per cell, so it's never off screen for long. Its `cells` are the waterline row under the hull: already sky (a barrier), so they matter only for what's rolled into them.
- **Cannon shot.** Every `volleyGaps` seconds it fires `volleySizes` shots (1, 2, 3 by lives lost), staggered gun by gun, at cells around `aimAhead(by: CannonShot.warningDuration)` that `GameManager.canCannonLand` allows: water with nothing solid (rock, barrels, cannonballs) above it all the way up, since a ball sinks straight down. So overhangs are shelter. A `CannonShot` is driven by its own age: a muzzle flash and smoke at the nearest gun port, the 3×3 patch glowing red (the tentacle-strike style), the ball arcing out, splashing in and sinking to the patch's middle, then landing at `warningDuration`. **Landing is the only moment it hurts: the head anywhere in the 3×3** (`covers`), and bomb-eater doesn't help. `GameManager.resolveCannonShot` also sets off bombs in the patch (`setOffBombs(in:)`, the same as a sensor's blast: mines go off, sensors arm, barrels detonate), then the ball comes to rest there as an ordinary `Cannonball` if the cell is clear and fewer than `maxLooseCannonballs` (6) are lying around; otherwise it breaks up.
- **Damage**: its own shot, rolled back up. A cannonball pushed up into the hull `smash`es into it (gone from `cannonballs` at once, so the snake moves into the cell it left), and the hit lands as it arrives: `cannonHits` holds a countdown and a point per ball, and `updateCannonHits` sets off an explosion there and calls `strike(galleon)`. A barrel blast up a column through the hull (`isCrossed`) also counts. `Galleon.maxLives` = 3, `hitInvulnerability` 1.5 s (a ball that lands during it is spent for nothing). Each hit flashes it and makes it heave. At zero it founders: its shots are called off, and it rolls over and sinks with a great splash, bubbling.
- **Drawing**: `SpriteArt.galleon` is one side-on texture (facing right; `xScale` flips it to come about) whose `galleonAnchor` puts the sprite's position at the hull's middle on the waterline; `galleonGunPorts` say where the shots come from. `animate(time:)` bobs and rolls it on the swell, harder after a hit.
- **Fixtures**: two barrels, three cannonballs in columns clear to the surface, and one starting mine.
